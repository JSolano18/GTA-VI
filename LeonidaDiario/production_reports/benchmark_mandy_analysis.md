# What may have driven the Mandy Short (~4,500 views vs ~20 normal)

Reported results: ~4,500 views, 56% stayed to watch, ~40 s average view duration on a ~40 s video, most views from the YouTube Shorts feed.

We have one data point, so none of these is proven. These are the factors that plausibly contributed, and how the new pipeline uses each one without copying the formula blindly.

| Possible factor | Why it may have mattered | How the new Shorts use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Real in-game footage of a character interaction** | It was the only Short with actual GTA VI gameplay of a specific scene people were talking about. The other Shorts used promo stills. | short_04 reuses the same mission with different segments. The others focus on Jason/Lucia interactions using images never shown on the channel. |
| **A named, specific moment** ("the Mandy scene", "Pipework") | Specific beats generic. Viewers searching or scrolling recognise it. | Every topic is one concrete thing: the relationship meter, True Fat Jason, Japan's shower edit, Lucia's bio, Mandy's choice. |
| **Curiosity gap that the video pays off** | "Is Mandy a killer?" kept people watching to the reveal. Average view duration ≈ full length. | Each Short opens on a question and closes on the answer plus an open question (no fake cliffhangers). |
| **Mildly suggestive but platform-safe visuals** | Mandy's outfit and the setting are attention-grabbing without breaking rules. | Used sparingly and only where the topic is genuinely about it (Japan shower edit). No thumbnails that misrepresent the content. |
| **Timing** | Published the morning after launch, while "Mandy" was trending. | All 5 topics come from coverage in the last 24–48 h, apart from the evergreen Lucia piece. |
| **Shorts-feed fit** | ~40 s, strong first frame, fast scene changes. | Kept 35–45 s, first line within 0.1 s, a new image every 5–8 s, word-synced captions. |

**Caveat:** a single outlier can also come from luck in the feed algorithm. Treat 56% retention and about 40 s average duration as the benchmark to compare the next 5 against, not as a guaranteed formula.
